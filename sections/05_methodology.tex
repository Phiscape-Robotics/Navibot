\subsection{Hardware and Electrical Design}
The motor-control system uses two Arduino Nano nodes: one for the front two motors and one for the rear two. Each custom controller board contains an Arduino Nano, an L298N dual H-bridge, and a UART-to-RS-485 interface module. The board receives 12 V; the L298N module's 5 V output is used to power other components. Confirm the exact L298N module/regulator arrangement, jumper configuration, available 5 V current, and connected loads against the schematic before relying on that rail. Exact part numbers, motor ratings, protection, grounding, and current budgets remain to be documented.

\textbf{RS-485 schematic discrepancy:} the project owner confirms that the RS-485 bus connects the front and rear motor-controller boards to the Raspberry Pi through its USB-RS-485 adapter. However, the supplied schematic marks transceiver A+ and B- as no-connect, which does not match the confirmed wiring. Update the EasyEDA schematic and exported PDF to show the bus connector and differential pair, then verify A/B polarity against the adapter and both nodes. The schematic also shows the +5 V rail at the Nano, encoder, and transceiver but does not show its regulator source; confirm that it is supplied by the L298N module's 5 V output and that the output can support the total load.

\subsection{Subsystem Configuration}
\subsubsection{Compute and Sensing}
The diagram depicts a Raspberry Pi 4B (4 GB), LiDAR, and camera, connected to an Arduino by UART. The confirmed design uses a USB-RS-485 adapter at the Pi and a shared bus with front and rear Arduino Nano nodes; the camera model is Pi Camera V2 (IMX219), and the serial LiDAR is a YDLIDAR X2. The ROS 2 driver has streamed scans on the Pi; mounting transform, forward-angle alignment, and ranging accuracy remain to be verified. The ROS 2 motor-bridge interfaces, camera bring-up, and sensor details are described in Section 6.

\subsubsection{Controller and Drive System}
The control system has two classic Arduino Nano nodes on the shared RS-485 bus. Each node is mounted on a custom board with an L298N and UART-to-RS-485 module and controls its respective pair of motors: front-left/front-right or rear-left/rear-right. The existing block diagram still shows one Arduino and must be revised to show both controller boards and the shared bus. Confirm from the schematic which encoder signals are wired to each board and how the nodes are powered.
